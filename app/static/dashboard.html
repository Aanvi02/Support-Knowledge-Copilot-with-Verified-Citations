How It Works

The pipeline that turns handbook pages into a checked answer. Names and thresholds below come from the project code.

  1. 1Knowledge documentsGitLab People Group handbook pages are the only source of truth for answers.
  2. 2Document ingestionEach page loses its front matter, is split by section heading, and long sections are split again. Very short pieces are merged into the chunk before them. Every chunk keeps its page title, heading and handbook URL.
    app/ingest.pydata/chunks.json
  3. 3Embedding and indexingEach chunk is turned into a vector that captures its meaning, then stored in a vector index for fast similarity search.
    Sentence Transformers: all-MiniLM-L6-v2FAISS
  4. 4Hybrid retrievalA question is matched two ways: by meaning (top 20 from the vector index) and by exact words (top 20 from BM25). The two rankings are merged with Reciprocal Rank Fusion and the best 5 chunks are kept.
    Semantic search + BM25RRF, k=60
  5. 5LLM answer generationThe language model writes an answer from the retrieved chunks only, splits it into claims, and marks each claim with the sources it relied on, like [1]. If the chunks don't cover the question, it says so instead of guessing.
    Model: loading…Groq API
  6. 6Citation verificationFor each claim, the text of its cited sources is cut into 3-sentence windows. A natural language inference model scores how strongly each window supports or contradicts the claim, and the best match becomes the evidence.
    cross-encoder/nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768
  7. 7Final answer with sourcesYou see the answer claim by claim, a verdict for each, the evidence passage that decided it, and every source that was retrieved, marked cited or not cited.

How a verdict is decided

VerdictRule
VerifiedBest support score is 0.50 or higher
ContradictedSupport is below 0.50 and the best contradiction score is 0.50 or higher
UnsupportedNeither score reaches 0.50, so the cited text neither confirms nor denies the claim
Bad citationThe claim cites a source number that doesn't exist
